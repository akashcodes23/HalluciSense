## 1. Executive Summary

HalluciSense Phase 6K successfully recovered, selected, and validated **Pillar 1 (External Evidence Support)** on the Development partition ($N=58,002$) and held-out Validation partition ($N=12,483$). The validated 5-feature Pillar-1 model (`Candidate 3`: `mean_entailment`, `max_entailment`, `mean_contradiction`, `min_support_margin`, `num_claims` + `RobustScaler` + `LogisticRegression(liblinear)`) achieved a held-out ROC-AUC of **$0.6259$**, PR-AUC of **$0.6388$**, MCC of **$0.1804$**, and ECE of **$0.0105$** ($1.05\%$) with zero numerical warnings.

However, **Pillar 1 relies entirely on external retrieved evidence $E$**. In real-world LLM deployments, external retrieval may be noisy, incomplete, or unavailable. Furthermore, language models frequently generate responses that are **internally self-contradictory, structurally incoherent, or logically incompatible**, even prior to external evidence checking.

**Phase 6L.0 introduces Pillar 2 (Structural Consistency)**. Pillar 2 evaluates the **internal coherence, intra-response claim compatibility, entity/attribute agreement, numerical consistency, and structural stability** of a generated response $R = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n\}$ **independently of external evidence $E$**. 

This specification establishes the formal mathematical model for Pillar 2 based on a **Claim Interaction Graph $G_R = (V, E_G)$**, defines 8 candidate feature families comprising 24 exact mathematical signals, analyzes the $O(n^2)$ pairwise computational complexity, specifies an immutable cache-first architecture, pre-registers evaluation metrics and baseline comparisons, defines the Phase 6L experimental firewall (DEV ONLY $N=58,002$), and identifies 12 threats to validity with concrete mitigations.

## 2. Pillar-2 Scientific Definition

### 2.1 Formal Conceptualization

Let $R$ denote a natural language response generated by a Large Language Model (LLM) in response to a user query $q$. The response $R$ is decomposed into a set of $n$ atomic claims:
$$R = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n\}$$

- **Pillar 1 (External Evidence Support)** models the mapping:
  $$\mathcal{P}_1(R, E) \in [0, 1]$$
  representing the degree to which claims in $R$ are supported or contradicted by external retrieved context $E$.

- **Pillar 2 (Structural Consistency)** models the mapping:
  $$\mathcal{P}_2(R) \in [0, 1]$$
  representing the degree to which the claims within $R$ are **mutually compatible, structurally coherent, entity-consistent, numerically non-contradictory, and logically plausible**, evaluated **without reference to external evidence $E$**.

### 2.2 Distinction Between Factual Incorrectness and Internal Inconsistency

Pillar 2 explicitly decouples **internal consistency** from **external factual truth**. A generated response can be classified across two orthogonal axes:

```
                          Factually Correct (External)      Factually Incorrect (External)
                        +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  Internally Consistent |  Quadrant A: Valid Truth       |  Quadrant B: Plausible Falsehood|
                        |  (P1 High, P2 High)            |  (P1 Low, P2 High)             |
                        +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
Internally Inconsistent |  Quadrant C: Flawed Truth      |  Quadrant D: Degenerate Error   |
                        |  (P1 High, P2 Low)             |  (P1 Low, P2 Low)              |
                        +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
```

1. **Quadrant A (Factually Correct + Internally Consistent)**: The response is accurate and coherent. (Low hallucination risk).
2. **Quadrant B (Factually Incorrect + Internally Consistent)**: The response is smoothly generated and coherent, but factually false relative to real-world evidence. **Pillar 1 detects Quadrant B**; Pillar 2 cannot detect it without external evidence.
3. **Quadrant C (Factually Correct + Internally Inconsistent)**: The response contains correct factual assertions mixed with internal self-contradictions (e.g., correct entity facts combined with conflicting dates or attributes elsewhere in the text). **Pillar 2 detects Quadrant C**.
4. **Quadrant D (Factually Incorrect + Internally Inconsistent)**: The response exhibits severe generation failure, internal self-contradictions, and factual error. **Both Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 detect Quadrant D**.

**Scientific Insight**: Pillar 2 is specifically designed to target Quadrants C and D. Because Pillar 1 targets Quadrants B and D, combining Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 via late fusion in Phase 6M will cover all four quadrants, providing complementary coverage across the hallucination spectrum.

## 4. Formal Mathematical Framework

### 4.1 Claim Interaction Graph Definition

Let a response $R = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n\}$ be represented as a weighted, directed multigraph:
$$G_R = (V, E_G, W)$$
where:
- **Vertices ($V$)**: $V = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n\}$, representing the $n$ atomic claims decomposed from response $R$.
- **Directed Edges ($E_G$)**: Ordered pairs $(c_i, c_j) \in V \times V$ with $i \neq j$.
- **Edge Weight Tensor ($W$)**: For each directed pair $(c_i, c_j)$, $W(c_i, c_j)$ is a 3-dimensional Natural Language Inference (NLI) probability vector:
  $$\mathbf{p}_{ij} = \begin{bmatrix} P_{\text{entail}}(c_i \to c_j) \\ P_{\text{neutral}}(c_i \to c_j) \\ P_{\text{contradict}}(c_i \to c_j) \end{bmatrix}$$
  satisfying $P_{\text{entail}}(c_i \to c_j) + P_{\text{neutral}}(c_i \to c_j) + P_{\text{contradict}}(c_i \to c_j) = 1.0$.

### 4.2 Contradiction & Entailment Adjacency Matrices

From the tensor $W$, we define two $n \times n$ directional adjacency matrices with zero diagonals ($C_{ii} = 0, E_{ii} = 0$):

1. **Directed Contradiction Matrix ($\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$)**:
   $$C_{ij} = P_{\text{contradict}}(c_i \to c_j) \quad \text{for } i \neq j, \quad C_{ii} = 0$$

2. **Directed Entailment Matrix ($\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$)**:
   $$E_{ij} = P_{\text{entail}}(c_i \to c_j) \quad \text{for } i \neq j, \quad E_{ii} = 0$$

### 4.3 Symmetrical vs. Asymmetrical Formulations

Because NLI model predictions on claim pairs $(c_i, c_j)$ can be directionally asymmetric due to premise-hypothesis formatting, we evaluate three formal scalar summaries for pair conflict:

1. **Symmetric Maximum Contradiction ($\mathbf{C}^* \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$)**:
   $$C^*_{ij} = \max\left(C_{ij}, C_{ji}\right)$$
   *Rationale*: If claim $c_i$ contradicts claim $c_j$ in *either* direction, an internal contradiction exists.

2. **Symmetric Mean Contradiction ($\mathbf{C}^{\text{mean}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$)**:
   $$C^{\text{mean}}_{ij} = \frac{C_{ij} + C_{ji}}{2}$$

3. **Asymmetric Directional Flow ($\Delta \mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$)**:
   $$\Delta C_{ij} = |C_{ij} - C_{ji}|$$

*Recommended Primary Matrix*: **Symmetric Maximum Contradiction $\mathbf{C}^*$**, as any single-direction contradiction signals an internal structural flaw.

## 5. Candidate Structural Signal Families

We formally define **8 structural feature families** containing **24 exact mathematical signals**.

### 5.1 Family A: Pairwise Claim Contradiction (5 Features)

Measures direct logical incompatibility between claims within the response. Let $M = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ be the total number of unique claim pairs for $n \ge 2$.

1. `mean_pairwise_contradiction`:
   $$\text{F1: } \bar{C}^* = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} C^*_{ij}$$
2. `max_pairwise_contradiction`:
   $$\text{F2: } C^*_{\max} = \max_{1 \le i < j \le n} C^*_{ij}$$
3. `p95_pairwise_contradiction`: 95th percentile of $\{C^*_{ij}\}_{1 \le i < j \le n}$.
4. `fraction_contradictory_pairs` (threshold $\tau = 0.50$):
   $$\text{F4: } \Phi_{\text{contradict}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} \mathbb{I}\left(C^*_{ij} \ge 0.50\right)$$
5. `contradiction_pair_count`:
   $$\text{F5: } N_{\text{contradict}} = \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} \mathbb{I}\left(C^*_{ij} \ge 0.50\right)$$

### 5.2 Family B: Claim Support / Agreement Structure (3 Features)

Measures intra-response claim consensus and mutual reinforcement.

6. `mean_pairwise_entailment`:
   $$\text{F6: } \bar{E}^* = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} \max\left(E_{ij}, E_{ji}\right)$$
7. `max_pairwise_entailment`:
   $$\text{F7: } E^*_{\max} = \max_{1 \le i < j \le n} \max\left(E_{ij}, E_{ji}\right)$$
8. `fraction_mutually_supportive_pairs`:
   $$\text{F8: } \Phi_{\text{support}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} \mathbb{I}\left(\max(E_{ij}, E_{ji}) \ge 0.70\right)$$

### 5.3 Family C: Semantic Redundancy & Repetition (3 Features)

Excessive repetition or paraphrasing signals LLM generation loops or decoding instability. Let $S_{ij} = \cos(\mathbf{v}(c_i), \mathbf{v}(c_j))$ be the cosine similarity between dense sentence embeddings $\mathbf{v}(c_i)$ and $\mathbf{v}(c_j)$.

9. `mean_pairwise_similarity`:
   $$\text{F9: } \bar{S} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} S_{ij}$$
10. `max_pairwise_similarity`:
    $$\text{F10: } S_{\max} = \max_{1 \le i < j \le n} S_{ij}$$
11. `near_duplicate_claim_fraction` (threshold $\theta = 0.85$):
    $$\text{F11: } \Phi_{\text{dup}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} \mathbb{I}\left(S_{ij} \ge 0.85\right)$$

### 5.4 Family D: Entity Consistency (3 Features)

Measures attribute and coreference conflicts across named entities mentioned in $R$. Let $\mathcal{E}(R)$ be the set of named entities extracted from $R$. For each entity $e \in \mathcal{E}(R)$, let $A(e, c_i)$ be the set of attributes/relations asserted about $e$ in claim $c_i$.

12. `entity_conflict_count`: Count of entity attribute pairs $(e, a_1, a_2)$ where $a_1$ and $a_2$ are mutually exclusive (e.g., birthplace = "London" vs "Paris").
    $$\text{F12: } N_{\text{entity\_conflict}} = \sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}(R)} \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} \text{Conflict}(A(e, c_i), A(e, c_j))$$
13. `entity_conflict_ratio`:
    $$\text{F13: } R_{\text{entity\_conflict}} = \frac{N_{\text{entity\_conflict}}}{\max(1, |\mathcal{E}(R)|)}$$
14. `entity_attribute_disagreement_score`: Weighted semantic conflict score across coreferenced entities.

### 5.5 Family E: Numerical Consistency (3 Features)

Detects incompatible numeric assertions within shared semantic contexts. Let $\mathcal{N}(c_i) = \{(v_{ik}, u_{ik}, t_{ik})\}$ be the set of numerical triples (value, unit, entity context) in claim $c_i$.

15. `numeric_conflict_count`: Count of numeric pairs $(v_1, v_2)$ attached to the exact same entity and unit context where $v_1 \neq v_2$ (e.g., "75 kWh" vs "52 kWh" for same vehicle battery).
16. `numeric_conflict_ratio`:
    $$\text{F16: } R_{\text{num\_conflict}} = \frac{N_{\text{numeric\_conflict}}}{\max(1, \text{Total Numeric Context Pairs})}$$
17. `max_numeric_disagreement`: Maximum relative difference $\frac{|v_1 - v_2|}{\max(|v_1|, |v_2|)}$ for context-matched numbers.

*Constraint Rule*: Unrelated numerical quantities (e.g., battery capacity vs motor power) are **strictly isolated** via dependency context matching to prevent false conflict detection.

### 5.6 Family F: Temporal Consistency (2 Features)

Detects chronological impossible orderings or date conflicts. Let $T(c_i)$ be temporal expressions or timestamps extracted from claim $c_i$.

18. `temporal_conflict_count`: Count of temporal order violations (e.g., event A occurring in 2024 described as occurring prior to event B in 2022 when event B is stated as A's prerequisite).
19. `timeline_order_violation_score`: Normalised temporal conflict ratio across temporal claims.

### 5.7 Family G: Claim Graph Topological Properties (3 Features)

Topological graph metrics computed on the thresholded contradiction graph $G^* = (V, E^*_{\tau})$ where $E^*_{\tau} = \{(c_i, c_j) \mid C^*_{ij} \ge 0.50\}$.

20. `contradiction_graph_density`:
    $$\text{F20: } D_C = \frac{|E^*_{\tau}|}{M} = \Phi_{\text{contradict}}$$
21. `max_contradiction_degree`: Maximum degree of any node in $G^*$:
    $$\text{F21: } \deg_{\max}(G^*) = \max_{i \in V} \sum_{j \neq i} \mathbb{I}\left(C^*_{ij} \ge 0.50\right)$$
22. `largest_contradictory_component_ratio`: Ratio of vertices in the largest connected component of $G^*$ to total claim count $n$.

### 5.8 Family H: Response Structural Controls (2 Features)

Structural baseline controls for model regularization and confounding adjustments.

23. `num_claims`: Total atomic claim count $n$ in response $R$. *(Note: Shared with Pillar 1; tracked for orthogonality analysis)*.
24. `claim_length_variance`: Variance of character lengths across atomic claims $\{|c_1|, |c_2|, \dots, |c_n|\}$.

## 7. Pairwise Computational Complexity Analysis & Optimizations

### 7.1 Quadratic Pair Complexity

For a response $R$ decomposed into $n$ atomic claims, the number of unique pairs evaluated is:
$$M = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} = \mathcal{O}(n^2)$$

#### Computational Bottleneck Estimates across Dataset Scale

| Claim Count ($n$) | Unique Pairs ($M$) | Directional NLI Passes ($2M$) | Est. Runtime (CPU @ 50ms/pass) | Est. Runtime (GPU Batch=64 @ 1ms/pass) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $n = 3$ | 3 | 6 | $0.30$ s | $0.006$ s |
| $n = 5$ | 10 | 20 | $1.00$ s | $0.020$ s |
| $n = 10$ | 45 | 90 | $4.50$ s | $0.090$ s |
| $n = 20$ | 190 | 380 | $19.00$ s | $0.380$ s |
| **DEV Partition ($N=58,002$, Avg $n=3.42$)** | **$\approx 261,000$ pairs** | **$\approx 522,000$ passes** | **$\approx 7.25$ hours** | **$\approx 8.7$ minutes** |

### 7.2 Pre-Registered Computational Optimizations for Phase 6L.1

To ensure scalable execution on DEV ($N=58,002$), Phase 6L.1 will implement a **4-stage optimization pipeline**:

1. **Upper-Triangular Pair Indexing**: Only evaluate unique pairs $i < j$ ($M$ pairs instead of $n^2$).
2. **Dense Embedding Pre-Screening Filtering**:
   Compute fast sentence embeddings $\mathbf{v}(c_i)$ using `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`. Skip expensive NLI evaluation for pair $(c_i, c_j)$ if $\cos(\mathbf{v}(c_i), \mathbf{v}(c_j)) < \theta_{\text{sim\_min}} = 0.15$ (unrelated claims have near-zero contradiction probability).
3. **Batch Vectorized NLI Execution**: Group candidate claim pairs across samples into dynamic batches of size $B = 64$ or $128$ for high-throughput GPU/MPS inference.
4. **Content-Addressable Persistent Caching**: Store pairwise NLI results in immutable disk caches keyed by $\text{SHA256}(c_i \parallel c_j)$.

## 8. Pillar-1 / Pillar-2 Conceptual Orthogonality Analysis

We construct a formal **Conceptual Overlap Matrix** comparing proposed Pillar-2 signal families against existing Pillar-1 features (`mean_entailment`, `max_entailment`, `mean_contradiction`, `min_support_margin`, `num_claims`).

```
===================================================================================================
                       PILLAR-1 / PILLAR-2 CONCEPTUAL OVERLAP MATRIX
===================================================================================================
Pillar-2 Signal Family            P1 Evidence Features     P1 Length (num_claims)   Expected Overlap
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Family A: Claim Contradiction     LOW (Evidence vs Claim)  MODERATE (Scale)        LOW
Family B: Claim Support           LOW (Evidence vs Claim)  MODERATE (Scale)        LOW
Family C: Semantic Redundancy     LOW                      LOW                      LOW
Family D: Entity Consistency      LOW                      LOW                      LOW
Family E: Numerical Consistency   LOW                      LOW                      LOW
Family F: Temporal Consistency    LOW                      LOW                      LOW
Family G: Graph Topology          LOW                      MODERATE (Graph Size)   LOW-MODERATE
Family H: Response Controls       LOW                      HIGH (Shared Feature)   HIGH
===================================================================================================
```

### Orthogonality Rationale
- **Families A & B**: Pillar 1 measures $P(E \to c)$, whereas Family A measures $P(c_i \to c_j)$. An LLM can generate claims that are strongly supported by evidence $E$ while being internally contradictory (Quadrant C), or internally consistent while unsupported by $E$ (Quadrant B). Expected linear correlation $r < 0.25$.
- **Families C, D, E, F**: Measure specific structural mechanisms (repetition, entity attributes, numeric units, timestamps) completely absent from Pillar 1's general NLI feature space. Expected linear correlation $r < 0.15$.
- **Family H (`num_claims`)**: `num_claims` is explicitly shared with Pillar 1. **Fusion protocol rule**: `num_claims` will be controlled for during multivariable modeling to prevent double-counting length bias.

## 9. Label Semantics & Weak Supervision Analysis

### 9.1 The Label Mismatch Problem

The ground-truth targets in `dev.jsonl` ($N=58,002$) and `val.jsonl` ($N=12,483$) represent **Global Hallucination Labels** ($y \in \{0, 1\}$). 

- A label $y = 1$ indicates that the response contains at least one factual or structural error.
- **Problem**: Many hallucinated responses ($y = 1$) belong to Quadrant B (factually incorrect relative to real-world knowledge, but fluently and consistently generated). For Quadrant B samples, structural consistency features will accurately report high consistency ($P_2 \approx 1.0$), creating a apparent "mismatch" with the global label $y = 1$.

### 9.2 Research Recommendation for Phase 6L

1. **Do NOT alter or split the dataset ground-truth labels**.
2. **Train Pillar 2 directly on DEV ($N=58,002$) using supervised Logistic Regression / Regularized Classifiers** as a standalone predictor, but expect lower raw ROC-AUC compared to Pillar 1.
3. **Formulate Pillar 2 as an Unsupervised / Weakly Supervised Structural Anomaly Index**:
   $$\text{Score}_{\text{P2}}(R) = 1.0 - \max\left(\bar{C}^*, C^*_{\max}, R_{\text{entity}}, R_{\text{numeric}}\right)$$
4. **Primary Validation Metric for Pillar 2**: Measure **incremental predictive gain ($\Delta\text{ROC-AUC}$, $\Delta\text{MCC}$, Net Reclassification Improvement)** when combining Pillar 2 with frozen Pillar 1 predictions on DEV CV, rather than requiring Pillar 2 to achieve high standalone ROC-AUC.

## 10. NLI Model Reuse Strategy Analysis

HalluciSense currently features a production DeBERTa-v3-large NLI engine located in [`app/core/engine/entailment.py`](file:///Users/akashgpatil/major_project/backend/app/core/engine/entailment.py).

### 10.1 Trade-off Analysis: Reuse vs. Dedicated NLI Model

```
Option A: REUSE EXISTING NLI MODEL (DeBERTa-v3-large)
  + Advantages: Zero additional GPU memory footprint; identical probability calibration; shared model weights.
  - Risks: If DeBERTa-v3-large has specific systematic biases (e.g. over-predicting neutral), both Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 will inherit correlated errors.

Option B: DEDICATED CONSISTENCY NLI MODEL (e.g. RoBERTa-large-MNLI or MiniLM-NLI)
  + Advantages: Independent error distribution; reduced correlated noise between P1 and P2.
  - Risks: Doubles memory requirement; requires second inference pipeline.

Option C: EXPERIMENTAL COMPARISON IN PHASE 6L.1 (RECOMMENDED)
  * Evaluate Option A vs Option B on DEV 1,000-example subset before freezing Phase 6L.1 feature extraction.
```

*Recommendation*: **Adopt Option C**. Begin with Option A (`app/core/engine/entailment.py`) for primary pipeline build, and run an experimental check against Option B during Phase 6L.1.

## 16. Persistent Cache Architecture

To prevent redundant, computationally expensive GPU/CPU inference, Phase 6L implements a **cache-first, content-addressable storage architecture** in `evaluation_results/phase6l/cache/`:

```
evaluation_results/phase6l/cache/
├── claim_pairs_index_dev.json    # Pre-computed claim pair indices for DEV
├── claim_pairs_index_val.json    # Pre-computed claim pair indices for VAL
├── pairwise_nli_dev.joblib       # Dense tensor C_ij, E_ij for DEV (SHA256 validated)
├── pairwise_nli_val.joblib       # Dense tensor C_ij, E_ij for VAL (SHA256 validated)
├── entity_numeric_cache_dev.json # Extracted entity/numeric/temporal triples for DEV
├── entity_numeric_cache_val.json # Extracted entity/numeric/temporal triples for VAL
└── p2_feature_matrix_dev.joblib  # Final 24-feature unscaled numpy matrix for DEV
```

### Cache Invalidation & Fingerprinting Rules
- Every cache file includes a header with `dataset_sha256`, `feature_schema_version = "6L.1.0"`, `nli_model_name`, and `timestamp_utc`.
- Atomic writes (`.tmp` -> `.joblib` rename) prevent cache corruption during unexpected interruptions.

## 18. Threats to Validity & Mitigations

| # | Threat to Validity | Severity | Description | Mitigation Strategy |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **T1** | Quadratic Complexity Explosion | High | $O(n^2)$ pairs cause long extraction times on 58,002 samples. | Upper-triangular indexing + embedding similarity pre-screening ($\cos < 0.15$ skip) + batch GPU NLI. |
| **T2** | Global Label Mismatch | High | $y=1$ includes Quadrant B (internally consistent but factually false). | Focus evaluation on incremental predictive gain ($\Delta\text{AUC}$) over frozen Pillar 1 rather than raw standalone AUC. |
| **T3** | Correlated NLI Model Errors | Medium | Reusing DeBERTa-v3-large may inherit Pillar 1 error biases. | Pre-register experimental check against dedicated NLI model in Phase 6L.1. |
| **T4** | Entity Coreference Ambiguity | Medium | Pronouns ("he", "it") may obscure entity attribute conflicts. | Apply lightweight heuristic coreference resolution prior to entity conflict scoring. |
| **T5** | False Numeric Conflicts | Medium | Unrelated numbers ("75 kWh" vs "150 kW") flagged as conflicting. | Require exact entity and unit context matching before evaluating numeric differences. |
| **T6** | Response Length Confounding | Medium | Longer responses naturally have more claim pairs $M = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$. | Include `num_claims` as explicit control variable; use normalised density/ratio features. |
| **T7** | Over-fitting on DEV | Medium | Searching 24 candidate features may overfit DEV partition. | Perform 5-fold, 3-repeat CV ($15$ folds); strictly seal VAL partition until final lock. |
| **T8** | Feature Collinearity | Medium | Pairwise contradiction metrics ($\bar{C}^*, C^*_{\max}$) may be collinear. | Execute strict collinearity audit ($|r| \ge 0.90$ filtering) prior to model selection. |
