Abstract—Large Language Models (LLMs) generate fluent yet
factually incorrect text—a phenomenon termed hallucination—
posing critical risks in high-stakes domains. Existing detec-
tion systems produce binary outputs, fail to localize halluci-
nated spans, and rely on single signals. This paper presents
HalluciSense, a model-agnostic hybrid framework that uni-
fies retrieval-based verification, model-intrinsic confidence scor-
ing, and cross-consistency checking into a single interpretable
pipeline. We introduce the Hallucination Score (H-Score), a
continuous0–1metric that quantifies severity through learned
fusion of factual error, confidence gap, and consistency failure
components. HalluciSense achieves span-level localization via
score propagation to tokens and sentences. The framework is
designed to operate across GPT-4, LLaMA-3, Mistral-7B, and
Gemini without fine-tuning. We detail the three-pillar archi-
tecture, the H-Score fusion mechanism, and a comprehensive
evaluation protocol targeting TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and FEVER
benchmarks.
Index Terms—Large language models, hallucination detection,
confidence quantification, retrieval-augmented verification, span-
level localization
I. INTRODUCTION
Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-4, LLaMA-3,
and Gemini have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across
natural language processing tasks. However, these models
frequently generate text that is factually incorrect, inconsistent,
or entirely fabricated—a phenomenon widely termedhalluci-
nation[6], [24]. The severity of this problem is amplified in
high-stakes applications including healthcare diagnostics, legal
analysis, and educational tutoring.
Existing hallucination detection methodologies exhibit three
critical limitations.First, current systems such as SelfCheck-
GPT [25] and FActScore [26] produce binary hallucinated/not-
hallucinated labels without quantifying severity [1]. A re-
sponse containing minor temporal inaccuracies demands dif-
ferent intervention than one with fundamental factual contra-
dictions, yet binary systems treat both identically.Second,
most approaches evaluate responses at the document or sen-
tence level without pinpointing specific problematic spans [7].
This coarse granularity impedes targeted correction strategies.
Third, the majority of detectors rely on isolated signals:
either retrieval-based fact-checking [26], model confidence
scores [4], or self-consistency sampling [25]. Each signal
individually suffers from characteristic failure modes, and no
existing framework robustly fuses all three signal types [6].
This paper presentsHalluciSense, a confidence-aware hy-
brid framework designed to address these limitations through
the following contributions:
1) A unified three-pillar architecture combining retrieval
verification, confidence analysis, and consistency check-
ing into a model-agnostic pipeline.
2) TheH-Score, a continuous0–1severity metric com-
puted asH=α·FE+β·CG+γ·CF, whereα, β, γ
are learned weights and FE, CG, CF denote factual
error, confidence gap, and consistency failure scores,
respectively.
3) Span-level localization propagating pillar scores to in-
dividual tokens and sentences, enabling interpretable
heatmap visualization.
4) A comprehensive evaluation protocol targeting Truth-
fulQA, HaluEval, and FEVER benchmarks with F1,
AUC-ROC, Span-IoU, and Expected Calibration Error
metrics.
II. LITERATUREREVIEW
A. Retrieval-Based Verification
Retrieval-based methods leverage external knowledge
sources to verify generated claims. FActScore [26] decom-

poses biographies into atomic facts and verifies each against
Wikipedia, achieving fine-grained fact-level precision but
lacking confidence integration and severity quantification.
RAGTruth [23] introduced a word-level manually annotated
corpus for retrieval-augmented generation scenarios; fine-
tuned detectors achieved competitive performance against
GPT-4, though the framework penalizes true facts absent
in provided context. LUMINA [8] measures distributional
distance for context utilization across transformer layers, out-
performing prior utilization-based methods by up to13%
AUROC on RAGTruth and HalluRAG, but requires unre-
stricted access to internal transformer layers. ReDeEP [19]
analyzes Knowledge FFNs and Copying Heads to decouple
internal parametric from external retrieved knowledge, im-
proving RAG hallucination detection accuracy at the cost
of higher processing latency. LettuceDetect [18] employs a
ModernBERT-based token-classification model handling up
to8k tokens, achieving excellent example-level F1 at30–60
examples per second, though it is pre-trained primarily on
English. FRANQ [12] proposes distinct uncertainty quantifica-
tion techniques to estimate factuality based strictly on context
faithfulness, achieving more accurate factual error detection
than standard UQ methods, but requires computationally heavy
parallel processing.
B. Confidence-Based and Uncertainty Quantification Methods
Confidence-based methods exploit model-internal signals to
detect uncertainty indicative of hallucination. INSIDE [21]
introduces EigenScore for semantic consistency combined
with feature clipping to reduce overconfidence, achieving
significant performance improvements using internal state
consistency metrics on CoQA, SQuAD, NQ, and TriviaQA;
however, it is dependent on selection of optimal intermedi-
ate layers. Semantic Entropy Probes [20] train lightweight
linear probes on hidden states to approximate semantic en-
tropy efficiently, capturing hallucinations in short-form gen-
erations with massive computational savings, though perfor-
mance drops on long-form generations with highly inter-
dependent reasoning chains. Kang et al. [4] comprehensively
evaluate token-probability, output consistency, internal states,
and self-checking uncertainty quantification techniques on
TriviaQA, GSM8K, and FactScore-Bio, finding that output
consistency and internal state probing outperform traditional
token-probability methods; the study focuses purely on QA
tasks. Binkowski et al. [17] propose LapEigvals, utilizing
top-keigenvalues of Laplacian matrices derived from in-
ternal attention maps, achieving state-of-the-art performance
among attention-based methods across five diverse LLMs on
NQ-Open, TriviaQA, CoQA, SQuADv2, HaluEvalQA, Truth-
fulQA, and GSM8K; the method is incompatible with closed-
weight models. Liang and Wang [3] introduce lightweight
MLP neural probes on frozen LLMs with multi-objective
joint loss and Bayesian optimization, boosting precision on
TriviaQA by over270%; optimal probe insertion layers vary
across different model sizes.
C. Consistency-Based Methods
Consistency-based methods assess hallucination through
response stability. SelfCheckGPT [25] samples multiple re-
sponses to the same query and measures consistency, estab-
lishing a strong baseline but lacking factual grounding, severity
quantification, and span-level localization. A unified multiple
testing framework [10] combines semantic entropy, lexical
similarity, and spectral eigenvalue distributions through mul-
tiple hypothesis testing, achieving the highest AUROC across
CoQA and TriviaQA using zero-resource models; it depends
on high-quality calibration datasets to establish baseline non-
hallucination distributions. Efficient hallucination detection via
semantic clustering [2] employs adaptive sampling variance
thresholds combined with DeBERTa-v3-large architecture,
achieving state-of-the-art AUROC with significantly fewer
samples on CoQA, TriviaQA, TruthfulQA, and SimpleQA;
performance is heavily dependent on secondary auxiliary NLI
models. Statistically efficient semantic entropy estimators [15]
propose SE-Bayes and SE-Histogram estimators computed
over semantic generation clusters, achieving high AUROC
with minimal sampling budgets on NQ, SV AMP, SQuAD,
and TriviaQA; clustering requires reliable auxiliary language
models.
D. Hybrid and Multi-Signal Approaches
Hybrid approaches remain limited. Song et al. [5] propose
a unified framework using multi-path reasoning with segment-
aware temporalized cross-attention, outperforming state-of-
the-art methods on TruthfulQA, GSM8K, and TriviaQA by
pinpointing subtle semantic dissonances; multi-path reason-
ing introduces substantial inference latency. Obeso et al. [9]
employ LoRA-based linear probes predicting entity labels
from hidden model activations for real-time detection in long-
form generation, achieving high AUC for Llama-3.3-70B on
LongFact, LongFact++, HealthBench, TriviaQA, and MATH;
the approach is restricted to entity-level hallucinations. LLM-
Match [22] uses a retrieval-augmented generation module
leveraging BioBERT embeddings followed by fine-tuning an
open-source language model with a classification head, achiev-
ing a superior Macro-F1 score of0.86on n2c2, SIGIR,
and TREC CT benchmarks. To our knowledge, no exist-
ing framework simultaneously integrates retrieval verification,
confidence analysis, and consistency checking into a unified
model-agnostic pipeline with continuous severity scoring and
span-level localization.
E. Surveys and Benchmarks
Alansari and Luqman [6] provide a structured taxonomic
analysis of detection methods and RAG mitigation strategies,
identifying attention bottlenecks and low-resource limitations.
Huang et al. [24] establish a coherent framework separating
factuality and faithfulness hallucinations, analyzing data, train-
ing, and inference causes. Wang et al. [22] survey factuality
challenges across language and vision modalities, identify-
ing a critical lack of efficient fact-checkers. HalluLens [14]
introduces a comprehensive benchmark with PreciseWikiQA,

LongWiki, and NonExistentRefusal, clarifying extrinsic versus
intrinsic hallucinations through dynamic test generation. Faith-
Judge [13] proposes an LLM-as-a-judge framework combined
with the HHEM model to track hallucination rates, improving
automated evaluation over zero-shot baselines on FaithBench,
AggreFact, RAGTruth, and TofuEval-MB, though scalability is
constrained by API costs. Sardana [16] benchmarks real-time
reference-free models including Lynx and HHEM, finding they
consistently detect incorrect RAG responses with high pre-
cision on CovidQA, PubmedQA, DROP, and FinanceBench,
but suffer false positives on complex multi-hop reasoning
questions.
F . Research Gaps
Our systematic literature survey of26papers reveals six
persistent gaps [6]:(1)Missing hybrid approaches that fuse
retrieval verification with internal confidence signals;(2)Lack
of continuous hallucination severity scoring beyond binary
classification;(3)Lack of fine-grained token-level localization;
(4)Computational bottlenecks in LLM-as-a-judge pipelines;
(5)Inflexible knowledge conflict resolution between para-
metric and retrieved knowledge;(6)Multilingual evaluation
scarcity beyond English-centric datasets. HalluciSense directly
addresses gaps (1)–(3).
III. METHODOLOGY
A. Problem Formulation
LetMdenote an LLM that generates a responseR=
[r1, r2, . . . , rm]consisting ofmtokens given a user query
Q. We define a hallucination as any spanR[i:j]within
the response that is factually incorrect, inconsistent with
established knowledge, or internally contradictory. We address
three problems:(P1)Detection: determine whetherRcontains
at least one hallucinated span;(P2)Quantification: compute a
severity scoreS(R)∈[0,1];(P3)Localization: identify all
hallucinated spans{R[i k :j k]}Kk=1 with individual severity
scores.

H. Model-Agnostic Design
HalluciSense achieves model-agnosticism through modular
adaptation: the Retrieval Verifier requires no model access; the
Confidence Analyzer adapts to available signals (full logits,
top-kprobabilities, or none); the Consistency Checker uses
standard generation APIs. For black-box models with lim-
ited confidence signals, the framework automatically adjusts
weights to rely more heavily on retrieval and consistency.

B. Custom Data Collection
A custom annotation corpus is being constructed through
the following protocol:
1)Response Generation:Prompt GPT-4, LLaMA-3,
Mistral-7B, and Gemini on TruthfulQA and HaluEval
to collect10K+(Q, R)pairs.
2)Annotation:Three annotators label hallucinated spans
with type (factual, consistency, fabrication) and severity
1–5. Inter-rater agreement target: Cohen’s Kappa≥0.7.
3)Knowledge Base:Build FAISS index on Wikipedia
November 2024 dump (∼6M passages) for retrieval
pillar evidence matching.
4)Split:70% train / 15% validation / 15% test, stratified
by model and hallucination type.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper presented HalluciSense, a confidence-aware hy-
brid framework for detecting, quantifying, and localizing hal-
lucinations in Large Language Models. By unifying retrieval-
based verification, model-intrinsic confidence analysis, and
cross-consistency checking, HalluciSense addresses the critical
limitations of existing single-signal, binary-output approaches.
The introduced H-Score provides a continuous, interpretable
severity measure, while span-level localization enables precise
identification of hallucinated regions. The framework’s model-
agnostic design ensures compatibility across GPT-4, LLaMA-
3, Mistral-7B, and Gemini without fine-tuning. A comprehen-
sive evaluation protocol targeting TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and
FEVER has been established, with full empirical benchmark-
ing scheduled for Phase 2 of development.
